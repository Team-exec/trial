Price Reasonability Analysis

What is Price Reasonability?

Price reasonability is the process of verifying whether a quoted or proposed price for a government procurement item or service falls within an acceptable market range. Under the General Financial Rules (GFR) 2017, every procurement officer must satisfy themselves that the price being paid is fair and not excessive.

This system uses an AI model trained on 5,000+ product records and 2,000+ service records from real government procurement data to predict fair market prices and flag anomalies.

How the AI Evaluates Prices

01 Base Price Lookup — Fetches historical average for the same item from the dataset.
02 Feature Engineering — Accounts for vendor tier, location, source, quantity, delivery, and warranty.
03 ML Prediction — Random Forest model (R² ≈ 0.95) predicts the fair market price.
04 Recommendation — Price range, risk factors, and approval level as per GFR guidelines.

Price Reasonability Assessment Criteria

Within Range

Predicted price falls within ±15% of the AI-estimated fair price. The quoted amount is considered reasonable and can proceed for administrative approval without further scrutiny.

Slightly Above Range

Quoted price is 15–30% above the AI prediction. The procurement officer should negotiate with the vendor or request additional quotations from competing suppliers before proceeding.

Significantly Above Range

Quoted price exceeds 30% above the AI prediction. Flagged as potential price inflation. Requires mandatory comparison with GeM rates, L1 bids, and investigation before approval.

Key Factors Considered

🏷️

Vendor Tier

Tier 1 vendors add ~15% premium for quality. Tier 3 offer ~15% discount.

📍

Location

Metro cities cost 2–8% more due to logistics, taxes, and demand.

📦

Quantity

Bulk orders above 10 units qualify for 2–12% volume discount.

📜

Source Reliability

GeM Portal rates (0.95) weighted higher than Vendor Quotation (0.88).
